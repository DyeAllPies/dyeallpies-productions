Secondhand description (from search-engine summaries of the ASME listing, and its citation as
ref. [13] in Chopin & Kudrolli 2013, papers/chopin2013-helicoids-wrinkles-loops.pdf): the paper
studies instabilities of thin bodies under large twist using a fully nonlinear plate theory,
which predicts compressive lateral (transverse) membrane stresses that earlier weakly nonlinear
theory misses, and which can significantly shift the critical twist angle at which buckling
occurs. Two end conditions are considered — ends held a fixed distance apart, and a fixed
applied axial force during twist — with different buckling modes and critical twist angles for
each. Chopin & Kudrolli 2013 cite Mockensturm 2001 alongside Cranford & Buehler 2011 and Kit
et al. 2012 as the numerical-simulation sources for finite-tension transverse buckling and
localized loop formation, i.e. the theoretical precursor to their θ_T ≈ 4.4·(h/W)^{1/2}
transverse-buckling scaling (see data/02-twisted-ribbon-and-rod-contact.csv).
